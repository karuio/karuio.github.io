TheValueist 拆 SpaceX 芯片融资：标题写 320 亿，整个结构是 440 亿

他对照 8-K、KBRA 评级公告和定价报道，拆出约 400 亿美元 GPU 租赁的分档、收益率和残值风险归属：A-3 档 8.259%，比投资级指数宽约 231 个基点；A-1、D、E 档规模仍未公开。

中文改写

新闻标题说这是一笔 320 亿美元的债券发行。TheValueist 翻了 SpaceX 的申报文件和评级、定价材料后认为，文件讲的故事更精确，也更单薄：结构有文件记录，公开发行部分的定价有报道，而整个融资结构比这两个数字都大。

8-K 里写了什么：一份约 400 亿美元的融资租赁

SpaceX 2026 年 10 月 26 日提交的 8-K（第 8.01 项）披露，公司在 10 月 24 日签了一份有约束力的承诺函，与 Apollo Global Management 管理的一个实体做主融资租赁：

  • 出租方购买 GPU 机柜（pods）及相关设备，租给 SpaceX，用在 SpaceX 选定的数据中心；
  • 设备分批交付，每批有自己的租赁明细表，期限从起租日起约五年到五年零一个季度；
  • 租金按季预付：每期为该批融资成本的二十分之一，加上按固定年利率计算的未偿租赁余额利息，再加某些持续性成本和费用，固定利率在定价时确定；
  • 各明细表的本金支付合计预计约 400 亿美元；
  • 签署租赁需满足交割条件，预计 2026 年底前完成；明细表在设备交付时起算，预计交付在 2027 年上半年。

8-K 没有写发行信托的名字、票据分级，也没有任何价格。

发行人是谁，结构怎么搭

KBRA 2026 年 11 月 16 日的初步评级公告点出了发行人：Saturn Funding Trust，一家新成立、破产隔离的特殊目的实体，是 WHCO Intermediate Holdings LP 间接全资持有的子公司。WHCO 是一家已有的第三方资产支持贷款机构，与 SpaceX 无关联。

  • 信托为 SpaceX 的算力业务融资购买英伟达 Vera Rubin NVL72 GPU 设备。
  • 租约是三净租约（triple-net），且为“无论如何都要付”（hell-or-high-water）条款，按季付款，用于完全摊还债务。无论设备状况如何，SpaceX 都要付租金。按彭博报道的投资者推介材料，SpaceX 不为设备残值提供担保：租期结束时芯片值多少钱，风险落在信托和投资者身上。
  • 资本结构从上到下是 A-1、A-2、A-3 三档优先级有担保票据，然后是 B 档次级有担保票据，再往下是 D 档和 E 档。
  • KBRA 给约 272 亿美元的 A-2、A-3 档和 24 亿美元的 B 档初步评级 A-。
  • KBRA 表示，评级主要由企业锚定方也就是 SpaceX 本身决定，并把杠杆率持续高于 3.5 倍列为降级敏感因素。

EDGAR 上没有信托本身的申报。这是一个 144A 规则下的结构，发行备忘录不公开。

各档票据

  • A-1 档：优先级有担保，是整个结构里记录最少的一块。它只出现在 KBRA 不公开全文的初步评级报告里；KBRA 的公告没提它，也没给它评级；Apollo 的定价公告完全没有分档明细；定价报道里也看不到它的规模、期限或价格。定价前的报道提到，整体方案包括一笔由高盛牵头、准备向银行银团化的 83 亿美元优先级有担保贷款，但这笔贷款是不是 A-1 档，没有公开信息。
  • A-2 档：约 44 亿美元优先级有担保票据，2031 年 4 月到期，定价收益率 7.66%，KBRA 初步评级 A-。
  • A-3 档：251.7 亿美元优先级有担保票据，2033 年 4 月到期，定价收益率 8.259%，KBRA 初步评级 A-。
  • B 档：25.64 亿美元次级有担保票据，2033 年 4 月到期，定价收益率 8.36%，KBRA 初步评级 A-。它排在 A 档之后、D 和 E 档之前，靠 D、E 档的次级保护以及一项单独的无担保差额回收分配获得支撑。
  • D 档和 E 档：结构最底层，私人持有，规模和定价未公开。它们和 A-1 档一起，构成 440 亿美元总额里三个公开发行档之外的约 119 亿美元。

A-2、A-3 和 B 档是公开银团发行，A-1、D、E 档是私募。

定价：比投资级指数宽约 231 个基点

Apollo 和高盛 2026 年 11 月 20 日宣布，各档优先级和次级票据合计约 440 亿美元完成定价，超额认购并扩大了规模。贝莱德管理的基金和账户、高盛资产管理，以及 KKR 提供建议的资本账户做了锚定认购。高盛是唯一的结构安排方。

440 亿美元是整个结构，包括私募的几档。周四新闻里的约 320 亿美元，是按报道分成三档公开银团发行的部分。

按报道，约 260 亿美元在周四 A-3 档最后 60 亿美元推出之前就已有认购承诺。据彭博报道，这些票据有 CUSIP 代码，可以立即在合格机构投资者之间交易。TheValueist 认为，这个设计让它们进入公司债买家的视野，没有把它们留在私募市场。

A-2 和 A-3 之间大约 60 个基点的差价，来自期限差异，与受偿顺序无关：两档都是优先级有担保，初步评级相同，但 A-2 在 2031 年 4 月到期，落在约 2032 年就陆续摊还完的租赁明细表之内；A-3 到 2033 年 4 月，超出了明细表期限。A-3 规模 251.7 亿美元，接近 A-2 的六倍，是必须由市场消化的那一档。B 档虽然是次级，在同样 2033 年到期的情况下，收益率只比 A-3 高约 10 个基点。

A-3 的收益率对比当天美国投资级公司债平均收益率 5.95%，高出约 231 个基点。据彭博报道，这笔交易的定价比 SpaceX 自己 2026 年 6 月发行的债务宽约 100 个基点。

报道中的整体方案还包括：高盛牵头、准备向银行银团化的 83 亿美元优先级有担保贷款，以及 SpaceX 的 70 亿美元租金预付款，对应约 473 亿美元的 GPU 及相关设备。报道称，贷款方坚持融资购买的芯片要留在地面数据中心，不能部署到轨道上；Apollo 的公告也把这次建设描述为地面设施。

落在什么样的资产负债表上

SpaceX 截至 2026 年 9 月 30 日季度的 10-Q 显示，资产负债表上已有约 388 亿美元债务和融资租赁，其中 129 亿美元是与 Valor Equity Partners 设备租赁相关的关联方债务，按售后回租失败处理入账。承诺函签于 2026 年 10 月 24 日，在这个季度结束之后，所以这些数字里还没有这笔租赁；它的会计处理要到年报里才首次出现。

已知和未知

TheValueist 的结论是：申报文件记录了这项承诺，约 400 亿美元本金，五年期、完全摊还的租赁明细表，KBRA 称这些义务是绝对且无条件的，前提是满足 8-K 列明的交割条件。报道给出了定价：优先级资金 7.66% 到 8.259%，次级 8.36%，都明显宽于投资级指数，也宽于 SpaceX 自己的收益率曲线。

两边都还没披露的是：发行备忘录、各档票面利率，以及私募的 A-1、D、E 档规模。他认为，这个模板接下来要靠事实检验：会不会有第二个发行人照搬这个结构，利差又是多少。

跟踪 AI 算力融资的投资者，可以把这笔交易的几个数字当作基准：A-3 比投资级指数宽约 231 个基点，比 SpaceX 自身债务宽约 100 个基点，残值风险由投资者承担。下一笔类似的 GPU 租赁证券化出来时，可以拿这几项直接对比。

SPACEX'S CHIP FINANCING, AS FILED

$SPCX $ANT $NVDA $MU $SNDK $LITE SPACEX'S CHIP FINANCING, AS FILED

The headlines called it a $32 billion bond sale. The filings tell a more precise story, and a thinner one: the structure is documented, the syndicated pricing is reported, and the full stack is larger than either number.

WHAT IS FILED: THE LEASE

SpaceX's Form 8-K, filed October 26, 2026 (Item 8.01), discloses a binding commitment letter dated October 24, 2026 for a master finance lease with an entity managed by Apollo Global Management.

• The lessor acquires graphics processing unit pods and related equipment and leases them to SpaceX for use at data centers SpaceX selects. • Equipment is delivered in tranches, each under its own lease schedule with a term of approximately five to five and one-quarter years from its commencement date. • Rent is payable quarterly in advance: one-twentieth of the financed cost of the tranche, plus a fixed rate per annum on the outstanding lease balance, plus certain ongoing costs and expenses. The fixed rate was set at pricing. • Aggregate principal payments under the schedules are expected to total approximately $40 billion. • Entry into the lease is subject to closing conditions and is expected to close before year-end 2026. Lease schedules commence on delivery, expected in the first half of 2027.

The 8-K does not name the issuing trust, the note classes, or any price.

THE ISSUER AND THE STACK

KBRA's preliminary rating release, dated November 16, 2026, names the issuer: Saturn Funding Trust, a newly formed, bankruptcy-remote special purpose entity and an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of WHCO Intermediate Holdings LP, an existing third-party asset-backed lending entity unaffiliated with SpaceX.

• The trust finances NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 GPU equipment for SpaceX's computing operations. • The lease is triple-net and hell-or-high-water, with quarterly payments intended to fully amortize the debt. SpaceX owes the rent regardless of the condition of the equipment, and, per the deal's investor presentation as reported by Bloomberg, provides no guarantee of the equipment's residual value: the end-of-lease chip value risk sits with the trust and its investors. • The capital stack runs Class A-1, Class A-2 and Class A-3 senior secured notes, then Class B subordinated secured notes, then Classes D and E. • KBRA assigned preliminary A- ratings to approximately $27.2 billion of the Class A-2 and A-3 notes and $2.4 billion of the Class B notes. • KBRA states the ratings are driven primarily by the corporate anchor, SpaceX itself, and names leverage above 3.5x on a sustained basis as a downgrade sensitivity.

No filing by the trust itself appears on EDGAR. This is a Rule 144A structure; the offering memorandum is not public.

THE CLASSES

• Class A-1: senior secured, and the least documented piece of the stack. It is named only in KBRA's preliminary rating report, whose full text is not public. KBRA's release never mentions it and assigns it no rating, the Apollo pricing release gives no class breakdown at all, and no size, maturity or price for it appears in the pricing coverage. Pre-pricing reports described a Goldman Sachs-led senior secured loan of $8.3 billion for syndication to banks as part of the wider package; whether that loan is the Class A-1 has not been published. • Class A-2: approximately $4.4 billion of senior secured notes due April 2031, priced at a 7.66% yield, preliminary A- from KBRA. • Class A-3: $25.17 billion of senior secured notes due April 2033, priced at an 8.259% yield, preliminary A- from KBRA. • Class B: $2.564 billion of subordinated secured notes due April 2033, priced at an 8.36% yield, preliminary A- from KBRA. It sits below the Class A notes and above Classes D and E, whose subordination, together with a separate allocation of unsecured deficiency recoveries, supports it. • Classes D and E: the bottom of the stack, privately held, their sizes and pricing not public. Together with Class A-1 they account for the roughly $11.9 billion of the $44 billion total that the three syndicated classes do not.

The Class A-2, A-3 and B notes were the syndicated classes; Class A-1, D and E were placed privately.

THE PRICING

Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced on November 20, 2026 that approximately $44 billion of senior and subordinated notes had priced across all classes, oversubscribed and upsized, with funds and accounts managed by BlackRock, Goldman Sachs Asset Management and capital accounts advised by KKR as anchor commitments. Goldman Sachs was sole structuring agent.

The $44 billion is the whole stack, including the privately placed classes. The publicly syndicated portion was the approximately $32 billion in Thursday's headlines, in three classes, as reported:

Approximately $26 billion was committed before the final $6 billion of the Class A-3 was offered on Thursday. The notes carry CUSIPs and trade immediately among qualified institutional buyers, per Bloomberg reporting, which is the design choice that puts them in front of corporate bond buyers rather than leaving them in the private placement market. The roughly 60 basis points between the Class A-2 and the Class A-3 is maturity, not seniority: both are senior secured with the same preliminary rating, but the A-2 matures in April 2031, inside lease schedules that run off by about 2032, while the A-3 runs to April 2033, past them, and at $25.17 billion, nearly six times the size of the Class A-2, it was the tranche that had to clear the market. Class B, though subordinated, priced only about 10 basis points wide of the A-3 on the same 2033 maturity. The A-3 yield compares with a 5.95% average yield on US investment-grade corporate bonds the same day, a gap of about 231 basis points, and the deal priced about 100 basis points wide of SpaceX's own debt issued in June 2026, per Bloomberg reporting.

Also reported as part of the wider package: an $8.3 billion senior secured loan led by Goldman Sachs for syndication to banks, and a $7 billion lease prepayment from SpaceX, against approximately $47.3 billion of GPUs and related equipment. Lenders were reported to have pressed for the financed chips to stay in terrestrial data centers rather than be deployed in orbit; the Apollo release describes the buildout as terrestrial.

THE BALANCE SHEET IT LANDS ON

SpaceX's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 shows debt and finance leases of approximately $38.8 billion already on the balance sheet, of which $12.9 billion is related-party debt from equipment leases with Valor Equity Partners, accounted for as a failed sale-leaseback. The commitment letter is dated October 24, 2026, after this quarter ended, so nothing of the lease sits in these figures; its accounting treatment first appears in the annual report.

THE READ

The filed record documents the commitment: about $40 billion of principal on five-year, fully amortizing lease schedules, obligations KBRA describes as absolute and unconditional, subject to the closing conditions the 8-K names. The reported record prices it: senior money at 7.66% to 8.259%, subordinated at 8.36%, all of it well wide of the investment-grade index and wide of SpaceX's own curve. What neither record yet shows is the offering memorandum, the per-class coupons, or the sizes of the privately placed A-1, D and E classes. The template question is now empirical: whether a second issuer copies the structure, and at what spread.
