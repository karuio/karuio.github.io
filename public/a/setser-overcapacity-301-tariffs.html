Brad Setser：用“产能过剩”301 对所有国家加税是个错误，中国对欧出口激增也怪不到美国头上

全球顺差集中在中国和芯片出口经济体，芯片顺差来自短缺；普遍加税会稀释对中国的关税差。他同时反对欧洲的“转移效应”说法，认为中国汽车出口激增源于自身产能和政策。

中文改写

据《华尔街日报》报道，特朗普政府准备动用针对“产能过剩”的 301 调查，把关税重新抬回此前依据 IEEPA（《国际紧急经济权力法》）征收时的水平，而且对象是所有国家，不只中国。

Brad Setser 在一条 14 帖的串帖里说，这是一个严重的错误。

顺差在东亚，芯片顺差来自短缺

Setser 的出发点是一张贸易差额图：全球顺差眼下集中在中国和芯片出口经济体，日本和欧盟并没有大额顺差，加拿大和墨西哥也没有。按他的判断，对所有人一律加征，打错了对象。

他接着说，全球顺差高度集中在东亚。芯片出口经济体顺差暴涨，原因是芯片短缺，相对于美国飙升的需求供不应求，跟芯片“产能过剩”扯不上关系。

打击面太广，反而说不清中国的问题

Setser 认为，四处出击，而且理由常常很牵强，会让政府很难拿出一套前后一致的论证，说明中国存在系统性的产能过剩倾向。他举的例子是挪威：难道挪威有“三文鱼”产能过剩，而不是在鱼类生产上有比较优势？

他认为针对中国的论证是站得住的：地方各自扶持本地龙头企业，彼此缺乏协调，再加上廉价的定向信贷，导致一个又一个行业的投资远超任何对国内外需求的合理估计；在中国政府重点扶持的行业，新产品涌入新市场，让其他地方不敢投资。

可一旦对所有国家加税，把欧洲拉回 15%，把中国拉到 20% 以上再叠加原有关税，亚洲大部分经济体放在 15% 到 20% 之间，对中国的打击就被稀释了。他的理由是，按现在的汇率，中国更在乎的是自己和别国之间的关税差，而不是关税的绝对水平。

别跟着欧洲说“转移效应”

话锋一转，Setser 也提醒，不要附和欧洲常见的说法：美国对华关税把中国产品转移到了欧洲市场。这种说法贸易律师很容易提出，欧盟直到不久前的钢铁关税就是以此为依据，听起来也“像是真的”。但他认为，它把中国对欧出口激增的责任过多推给了美国，对中国自身的责任算得太少。

转移效应的前提，是美国关税在压低美国进口上很有效。Setser 认为实际效果没那么大：汽车和家用耐用消费品上有一些影响，但净效果远小于美国从中国直接进口的降幅。

在很多行业，美国消费者只是付了关税（所以才有关税收入），进口总量并没减少；在另一些行业，关税的主要作用是改变了最终组装地。他引用摩根士丹利的估算：即使加了关税，美国从中国进口的增加值总量也没怎么变，这就限制了可能被转移出去的规模。

在有人追问是否属于转口时，他在串帖下方补充说，与其说是转口（违法，虽然也确实存在，属于关税欺诈），不如说关税惩罚的是最终组装地，而不是零部件和增加值的来源地。

汽车：没有贸易可转，产能本身太大

他认为在汽车这样的行业，没有任何说得通的理由，把中国对欧盟乃至全世界的出口激增归因于美国政策，而不是中国政策。他给了两条理由。

第一，美国从 2018 年起就通过 301 条款对中国汽车加征关税，而且从来没从中国进口过多少汽车，本来就没有现成的贸易可以转移。

第二，中国汽车产能的规模太大。他写道，产能至少 5000 万辆，他怀疑更接近《华尔街日报》报道的 6000 万辆；今年中国国内需求又下降了大约 500 万辆。这样算下来，即使美国每年吸纳 100 万到 150 万辆，中国也还有大量产能可以出口到欧洲。

该抱怨什么

Setser 承认，抱怨美国关税有正当理由，它们确实在影响欧洲，比如欧盟车企正在到美国投资以绕开关税。抱怨中国的一系列政策更有充分理由：进口替代政策、对重点行业的地方补贴加廉价信贷，以及被低估的汇率。

所以在他看来，用中国出口激增来批评美国关税，在政治上和分析上都是错误。这轮激增主要源于中国自己的政策，而且早于特朗普的所有关税，他认为实际是从 2023 年开始的。

怎么读这轮关税

如果你在跟踪 301 关税对中国和亚洲出口链的影响，按 Setser 的思路，可以盯两个数：中国和其他经济体之间的关税差有没有被拉平；中国出口量和进口量的差距是否还在扩大。前者决定关税对中国还有多大约束力，后者决定贸易摩擦的源头在哪里。以上判断都是 Setser 的观点，相关关税方案以官方最终公布为准。

Overcapacity 301 tariffs on everyone are a profound mistake (thread)

It looks like the Trump administration is poised to use the overcapacity 301s to raise tariffs back to their "IEEPA" levels on everyone -- and not just on China.

That is a profound mistake. The global surplus right now is in China and the chip exporters -- and not in Japan and the EU (or for that matter Canada and Mexico)

1/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125366780059385882

The global surplus right now is heavily concentrated in East Asia -- and the surge in the surplus of chip exporters is due to a chip shortage (relative to surging US demand) not chip "overcapacity" ...

2/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125367389705408933

Going after everyone, often on flimsy grounds (Norway has "salmon" overcapacity & not a comparative advantage in fish production?), makes it hard for the administration to make a coherent intellectual case that China has a systemic tendency towards overcapacity ....

3/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125367790655684629

I think the case can be made for China -- uncoordinated regional support for local champions + cheap/ directed credit lead to investments that far exceed any reasonable estimate of demand (domestic and global) in sector after sector, and flood new markets in sectors targeted by the Chinese government in a way that deters investment elsewhere

4/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125368116075245682

But going after everyone to get tariffs back up to 15% (Europe) and 20% plus legacy tariffs (China) and between 15-20% for most of Asia dilutes the impact of the tariffs on China (at current exchange rates China care more about the tariff differential than the absolute level)

5/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125368948048679254

That said, I would be careful not to echo the standard European argument that US tariffs on China have diverted Chinese production in to the European market. That is an easy argument for trade lawyers to make (it was the basis for the EU steel tariffs until recently) and "feels" true ... but it puts too much blame on the US for the surge in China's exports to the EU and not enough blame on China itself

6/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125369331802366393

The diversion argument assumes that the US tariffs have been more effective at reducing US imports than I think they have been

(there is some effect on autos and household consumer durables, but the net effect is much smaller than the reduction in direct US imports from China)

7/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125369595997389066

Remember that in many sectors US consumers have just paid the tariff (hence the revenue) and not reduced total imports -- and in others the main effect of the tariff has been to change the location of final assembly

8/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125369820296135062

Morgan Stanley has estimated that the total value added the US imports from China hasn't changed much, even with the tariffs -- which reduces the extent of potential diversion

9/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125369994980872291

And in sectors like autos there is no plausible case that the surge in Chinese exports to the EU (and the world) is a function of US policies rather that Chinese policies

10/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125370206906372299

For one, the US has tariffed Chinese autos since 2018 (via the 301) and never imported any cars to speak of from China, so there isn't any existing trade to divert

11/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125370629171933546

And the scale of China's auto capacity (at least 50m cars, and I suspect more like the 60m reported by the WSJ) is so big and the fall in Chinese internal demand this year is so great (5m cars or so) that China would have tons of capacity to export to Europe even if the US was taking in 1 to 1.5m cars ...

12/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125371009440133224

There are legitimate grounds to complain about the US tariffs, which are having an impact on Europe (EU auto firms are investing in the US to get around the tariffs) -- and very legitimate grounds to complain about a host of Chinese policies (import substitution policies, local subsidies + cheap credit to favored sectors, and undervalued currencies) ....

13/

Thread post, https://x.com/Brad_Setser/status/2125371600971383185

So in my view it would be both a political and analytical mistake to criticize the US tariffs from a surge in Chinese exports that stems much more from China's own policies ... and that in fact predates all of Trump's tariffs (it really started in 2023 in my view)
