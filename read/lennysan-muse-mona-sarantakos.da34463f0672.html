Lenny 访谈 Muse 产品负责人：10 人团队花七个月打磨信任，系统提示词必须人写

Meta 旗下 AI 助手 Muse 的产品负责人 Mona Sarantakos 讲了原型在董事会前崩溃、购物成了意外的头号场景、用代理群分析对话，以及她招 PM 先看品味。

中文改写

Mona Sarantakos 是 Meta 旗下 AI 助手 Muse 的产品负责人，也是 Meta 超级智能实验室（Meta Superintelligence Labs）的产品副总裁。Lenny Rachitsky 称 Muse 是今年最成功的 AI 消费产品，在他引用的上一条帖子里，他写的是“史上增长最快的 AI 消费产品”。这是 Mona 第一次接受播客采访，Lenny 把访谈整理成了十条要点。

原型在董事会前一小时崩了

首席设计师 Alex（@AlexCornell）用几天时间做出了第一个能跑的原型，团队临时决定拿到董事会上展示。会前一小时，原型崩了。Alex 和一位工程师抢修，在会议开始前五分钟恢复。Mona 说：“在某个平行宇宙里，我们没搞定，Muse 可能就不会诞生。”

吉祥物 Jolly 原本只是占位符

如今已成标志的 Muse 形象 Jolly，最初只是个占位符。Alex 为第一个原型画了这个角色，叫它 Jolly Bot，名字来自他给女儿起的昵称。每隔几个月就有人说，不能拿一个毛绒玩具当 logo 上线。Mona 的说法是：“Jolly 就像我们的蟑螂，怎么都死不了。”上线前一周，团队和马克·扎克伯格拍板：Jolly 留下。

她的理由是：和一个感觉如此私人的东西对话时，人最不想面对的就是一个企业 logo。

起点是 Nat Friedman 的一条消息

一月下旬的一个周四，Nat Friedman 在 Meta 内部给几个人发消息，说 OpenClaw 正在改变他对“什么是可能的”的全部看法，大家得试试。

Mona 花了一个周末把它装在树莓派上（Mac Mini 卖断货了），给它开了自己邮箱、日历和家居自动化的权限。她丈夫当时说，这太不安全了。周一早上，它给她发来这一周的安排汇总，内容取自孩子学校那些她一向跟不上的邮件。她回复 Nat：“就是它了，我加入。”

最早动手，却用了七个月才上线

Muse 的工作比其他所有 AI 助手都开始得早，却花了七个月才上线。团队先想清楚产品是什么，几个月内就做出了完整可用的端到端版本，剩下的时间全花在可靠性、安全和信息安全上。内部口号是“Trust is a must”，意思是信任必不可少。

10 人核心团队，一日三餐一起吃

最初的 Muse 团队只有 10 个人。核心团队刻意保持小规模，让沟通和决策都很紧凑。他们在门洛帕克一栋楼里一块像图书馆的公共区域办公，每天三顿饭都一起吃。Mona 说：“那就像把闪电装进了瓶子。我想象不出这个产品能由别的团队做出来。”

购物成了没人预料到的头号场景

Mona 原本预测，主要场景会是个人效率、健康和个人理财。也有人押家居自动化，这在 OpenClaw 用户里很流行。结果内部测试者开始拿它购物。工程经理 @christinesed 成了最积极的内部试用者，每天早上提交购物相关的 bug 报告。Mona 说，他们的评测原本全放在别的方向上，于是团队开始专门搭建购物评测。

用“代理群”读用户对话

Muse 的对话又长又是多轮，现成工具都分析不了。团队就派成群的定制代理去读匿名化后的对话和反馈。这些代理群回报哪些地方好用、哪些地方坏了，细到“有 10 个人想在某个网站购物却被拦下”这种程度。Mona 说：“这让我怀疑我们以前到底是怎么做产品的。”

系统提示词必须由人来写

Mona 的原则是：“系统提示词不能用 AI 写，任何一部分都不行。”团队在其他地方到处用代理，包括帮忙写评测。但数据始终要有人审，因为“一旦不审，我们总会出问题”。

招产品经理，她先看品味

她现在招 PM，第一看的是产品品味。她同意一个说法：过去两年 PM 这个角色的变化，比此前二十年加起来还大。她现在可以想到一个点子，自己动手做出来，再拿着它进行一场真正的讨论，整个过程“在 24 小时之内”。

在她看来，让 PM 脱颖而出的是三件事：代表客户，坚持高标准，以及能对一个爱上自己点子的工程师说：“这很酷，我很高兴你这么喜欢它。但我觉得没有别人会喜欢。”

下一步：从 App 走向随身设备

Muse 的下一个方向是“环境式存在”（ambient presence）。近期，Mona 想减少用户盯着代理干活的负担，她说：“如果我得一直看着代理，那还不如自己干。”她还想让 Muse 走出 App，进入眼镜和其他设备。

但她没有声称知道这条路会通向哪里：“一年前，我绝不会像今天这样向你描述 Muse。”

访谈里还有什么

Lenny 在引用的那条帖子里列出了访谈的其他话题：竞争对手都在他们之后起步，他们为什么还是等了七个月才上线；真正能拉动 AI 代理激活率的两个杠杆；Nat Friedman、Alexandr Wang 和 @finkd 各自在产品上留下了什么印记；团队为什么比起仪表盘更依赖代理群；以及为什么品味成了 PM 最重要的技能。完整访谈在 YouTube 上，本站没有看过视频，以上内容只来自 Lenny 的两条帖子。

My biggest takeaways from @mona_sarantakos, Head of Product for @Muse

My biggest takeaways from @mona_sarantakos, Head of Product for @Muse, the most successful AI consumer product of the year:

  1. The first Muse prototype broke an hour before the board meeting pitch. Lead designer @AlexCornell built the working prototype in a few days, and the team decided at the last minute to show it at a board meeting. An hour before, it broke. Alex and an engineer raced to fix it, and it came back five minutes before the meeting started. “In some alternate universe we didn’t figure this out and maybe Muse was never born.”

  2. Jolly, the now-iconic Muse avatar, was just a placeholder. Alex created the character for the first prototype and called it Jolly Bot, after his nickname for his daughter. Every few months, someone said they could never launch with a stuffed animal as the logo. “Jolly’s like our cockroach. It just never dies.” A week before launch, the team and Mark Zuckerberg made the call: “Jolly stays.” Mona’s reasoning: “When you’re talking to something that feels so personal, the last thing you want to talk to is a corporate logo.”

  3. Muse began with a ping from @natfriedman. On a Thursday in late January, Nat messaged a few people internally: OpenClaw was changing his whole view of what’s possible, and they needed to try it. Mona spent the weekend setting it up on a Raspberry Pi (Mac Minis were sold out) and gave it access to her email, calendar, and home automation. On Monday morning it sent her a summary of what was coming up that week, pulled from the school emails she never keeps up with. She pinged Nat back: “This is it.”

  4. Muse took seven months to launch, even though work started before every other AI assistant. After figuring out what the product was going to be, and building a fully functioning end-to-end version in a few months, the team spent the rest of the time on reliability, safety, and security. Their internal motto was “Trust is a must.”

  5. The original Muse team was just 10 people. The core team stayed small on purpose, to keep communication and decisions tight. They worked out of a library-like common area in a Menlo Park building and ate all three meals together each day. “It was like lightning in a bottle. I couldn’t have imagined this product being built with any other team.”

  6. The team didn't expect shopping to be one of Muse’s top use cases. Mona predicted that personal productivity, health, and personal finance would be the primary use cases. Others bet on home automation, which was big with OpenClaw users. Then internal testers started shopping. Engineering manager @christinesed became the top dogfooder, sending shopping bug reports every morning. “All of our evals have been somewhere else,” so the team started building shopping evals.

  7. The team uses “agent swarms” to surface user insights at scale. Muse conversations are long and multi-turn, and no existing tool could analyze them. So the team sends swarms of custom-built agents through anonymized conversations and feedback. The swarms report back what’s working and what’s breaking, down to the 10 people who tried to shop on one site and got blocked. Mona said, “It makes me wonder how we even built products before.”

  8. Muse’s system prompt has to be written by a person. “You cannot use an AI to write a system prompt, any part of it.” The team uses agents everywhere else, including to help write evals. But a human always reviews the data, because “when we don’t, we always have issues.”

  9. Product taste is now the first thing Mona looks for in a PM. She agrees that the PM role has changed more in the past two years than in the two decades before. She can now come up with an idea, build it herself, and have a real conversation about it “in a 24-hour cycle.” What sets a PM apart is representing the customer, holding a high bar, and telling an engineer who loves their own idea: “That’s really cool. I’m glad you really like it. I don’t think anyone else will.”

  10. The next frontier for Muse is ambient presence. Near term, Mona wants to cut the babysitting (“At the point where I’m babysitting an agent, I might as well do it myself”) and move Muse beyond the app into glasses and other devices. But she doesn’t claim to know where this goes: “A year ago, I would’ve never explained Muse to you the way that I did.”

In late January, @natfriedman pinged a few people at @Meta. He’d been using OpenClaw, and told them they needed to try it.

Mona Sarantakos couldn’t find a Mac Mini, so she spent the weekend setting it up on a Raspberry Pi and gave it access to her email and calendar. Her husband told her, “That is so not safe.”

But by Monday morning, it had pulled together everything her kids had going on at school that week. She pinged Nat back: “This is it. I’m in.”

The team spent the next seven months building something safe and simple enough for everyone. They called it @Muse. It became the fastest-growing AI consumer product ever.
