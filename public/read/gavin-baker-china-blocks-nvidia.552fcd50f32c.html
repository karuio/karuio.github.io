Gavin Baker：他说，拦下英伟达 GPU 的，越来越多是北京而不是华盛顿

他引用路透社关于约 60 万颗 H200 滞留香港的报道，并认为向中国出售非前沿芯片仍是对美国有利的政策。

中文改写

Gavin Baker 写给那些认为拜登政府和特朗普政府允许较弱 GPU 对华出口是错误的人。他想讨论的是：从 2025 年夏天开始，阻拦英伟达 GPU 进入中国的，越来越多是中国自己，而不只是华盛顿。

按他的叙述，商务部长 Lutnick 曾说，目标是让中国开发者“上瘾”于美国技术栈。其后数周内，北京开始施压大型科技公司停止采购 H20，接着要求它们停止采购 RTX Pro 6000D，派海关人员到港口查处英伟达芯片进口，并禁止新建的国家资助数据中心使用外国 AI 芯片。

至于数量，他转述路透社上周的报道称，大约 60 万颗 H200 滞留在香港，原因是北京不愿大规模批准进口这款芯片。在他看来，H200 在规模化产品上仍优于中国本国产业。上述数字和报道细节来自他的转述，本站未对照路透社原文逐句核对。

他反问：如果允许出口这些较弱 GPU 是一项让中国占便宜的政策错误，那为什么出面阻拦的是中国？

他自己的判断是，中国明白，一旦在 AI 硬件上依赖美国，无论芯片放在中国境内，还是通过东南亚云（其中包括美国超大规模云厂商在东南亚的可用区）远程调用，美国在未来冲突中都会握有很大杠杆。他认为，能够立刻关掉云，才是最大的杠杆。

他也承认，中国的 AI 实验室仍想要英伟达芯片。他写道，H200 服务器的灰色市场价格在过去一年大约涨到了三倍。可这正好说明问题：北京愿意给本国实验室施加实际成本，以避免依赖美国 GPU。在他看来，北京把 GPU 进口看成对自身雄心的威胁，而不是礼物。

因此他认为，向中国出售落后于美国前沿水平的 GPU，让中国开发者继续留在美国 GPU 体系上，并减少中国另建一套美国无法影响、日后还可能与对手分享的 AI 硬件生态，这其实是一项对美国有利的国防政策。让它们使用东南亚的云，在他看来可能更聪明，因为云很容易被关掉。

他强调，这是自己一贯的看法。他也说，自己在网上的对华立场已经偏鹰派，因此不再去中国旅行。

他还指出，拜登政府和特朗普政府都曾基于这些理由允许较弱 GPU 对华出口。按他的说法，中国是在特朗普任内才意识到这项政策对自己的风险，并开始限制 GPU。

H20 给他的教训是：一款被刻意削弱的芯片，并不能把中国留在美国的技术栈上。他认为，北京是在判断国产芯片已经追上 H20 和 RTX Pro 6000D 之后，才对这两款产品采取行动。所以他推测，如果中国日后发展出可与俄罗斯、伊朗和朝鲜分享的替代硬件生态，回头看会觉得 2025 年向中国提供 B30A，可能比后来逐案批准 H200 更聪明。他补充说，H200 已经不是前沿产品：在 Vera Rubin 已全规模投产的前提下，它比美国实验室正在采购的芯片落后两代。Vera Rubin 的投产进度是他的陈述。

他把这些点和黄仁勋的游说联系在一起：黄仁勋一直推动的立场，被两届美国政府采纳，而且在他看来，随着中国实验室越来越多把混合专家架构适配到国产硬件上，这个立场每个月都显得更站得住。他称，有报道说 DeepSeek 把 V4 的早期权限给了华为和寒武纪，而不是英伟达和 AMD；一个由华为牵头的团队此后还在昇腾芯片上对 V4-Pro 做了后训练。这些都是他转述的“据报”内容。

最后他说，如果美国将来在 AI 上落后于中国，那些在美国国内加速催生这套替代硬件生态的对华强硬派，应该承担相当一部分责任。这是他的政策评论，不是已经发生的事实。

Something that merits more discussion amongst those who believe it was a mistake by both the Biden and Trump administrations to allow the export of less powerful GPUs to China

Something that merits more discussion amongst those who believe it was a mistake by both the Biden and Trump administrations to allow the export of less powerful GPUs to China:

Since the summer of 2025, it has increasingly been China, not just DC, blocking Nvidia GPUs in China. Within weeks of Secretary Lutnick saying the goal was to get Chinese developers "addicted" to the American technology stack, Beijing began pressuring its tech giants to stop buying the H20. Then it ordered them to stop buying the RTX Pro 6000D, sent customs teams to the ports to crack down on Nvidia chip imports, and barred foreign AI chips from new state-funded data centers. And as of last week, Reuters reports that roughly 600,000 H200s are sitting in Hong Kong because Beijing won't approve large-scale imports of a chip that is still better than anything China's own industry makes at scale.

If allowing the export of these less powerful GPUs was a policy mistake that advantaged China, then why is China the one blocking them?

From my perspective, China understands that if they are dependent on American AI hardware - either on the ground in China or accessed remotely via the wide range of southeast Asian clouds (which include SE Asian availability zones from the largest American hyperscalers) - then we have immense leverage over them in any future conflict. Being able to turn off the clouds instantly is actually the greatest leverage.

Yes, Chinese AI labs still want Nvidia chips. Gray market prices for H200 servers have roughly tripled over the past year. But that is exactly the point. Beijing is willing to impose real costs on its own AI labs to avoid dependence on American GPUs, which tells you how Beijing views GPU imports: not as a gift, but as a threat to its own ambitions.

Maybe, just maybe, selling China GPUs that trail our own state of the art, so that Chinese developers stay “addicted” to American GPUs and China doesn't build an alternative AI hardware ecosystem that we have no control or influence over and that China could share over time with our adversaries, was actually a smart, pro-American national defense policy. And letting them access southeast Asian clouds might be an even smarter policy as so easy to turn clouds off.

I believed and still believed it was smart policy. And I say this as someone whose posts here are hawkish enough on China that I won't travel there anymore.

And note that both the Biden and Trump administration allowed the export of less powerful GPUs to China for all of these reasons. It was only under the Trump administration that China woke up to the risk of what was actually a pretty smart, pro-national defense policy and then China began restricting GPUs.

The lesson of the H20 is that a deliberately crippled chip doesn't keep China on our stack. Beijing moved against the H20 and RTX Pro 6000D as it concluded that domestic Chinese chips had caught up to them. So if China develops an alternative AI hardware ecosystem that it shares with Russia, Iran and North Korea, we will look back and think it would have been even smarter to offer China the B30A in 2025 instead of the H200s we eventually approved case-by-case. Especially given the H200 is not the frontier: with Vera Rubin now in full production, it is two generations behind what American labs are buying.

All of this is to say that Jensen has been consistently lobbying for a position that was adapted by both the Biden and Trump admins and looks smarter every month that goes by as Chinese labs increasingly adapt their MoE architectures to domestic Chinese hardware. DeepSeek reportedly gave Huawei and Cambricon early access to V4 instead of Nvidia and AMD, and a Huawei-led team has since post-trained V4-Pro on Ascend chips.

And if America ever falls behind China in AI, the China hawks here in America who helped accelerate the creation of this alternative Chinese AI hardware ecosystem will deserve a significant share of the blame.
