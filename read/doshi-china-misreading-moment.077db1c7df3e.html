中文改写

特朗普和习近平定于周四在深圳会面。会面前，Rush Doshi 在《外交事务》（Foreign Affairs）发表文章《中国是否误判了形势？》，副标题是“北京的过度扩张威胁到与美国的缓和”。他在 X 上用一条长帖概括了全文要点。

他的核心判断：两国一年前达成的所谓“缓和”，现在承受着很大的压力。三件事同时在起作用：釜山协议因为稀土出现裂痕；新一届由民主党控制的国会会制约特朗普；英伟达丑闻正在催生新的出口管制。平静了一年之后，接下来可能是一段紧张期。

北京把缓和期当成扩大优势的窗口

Doshi 认为，北京利用这段缓和期压实自己的优势：锁定产业领先地位，胁迫周边国家，让稀土供应保持偏紧，用这根绳子把美国拴住。同一时期，美国一直在迁就。

但他认为北京做过头了。如果美国认定不管自己怎么做，北京都会卡稀土，那继续克制就没有多少理由。贸易休战 1 月 10 日到期，两位领导人延长或维持休战的时间不多了。深圳这次会面，加上 12 月在迈阿密的下一次会面，是稳定局面仅剩的几次机会。

长帖接着列了 8 个要点，前三点引的是中国学者的说法。以下引语都是 Doshi 的英文转述再译回中文，并非中文原话。

中国学者眼中的缓和：临时的，靠实力换来的

**第一，中国学者把眼下的缓和看作临时安排。**陈东晓称之为“战术性休战”；吴心伯说这是“战术性稳定”，“既不可持续，也不牢固”。前外交部官员 Shen Weizhong 说：“我们的时间越多，就越能为台湾问题上的最终摊牌，或者再来一场贸易战，做好准备。”

**第二，他们认为缓和是中国的实力打出来的，并非外交谈出来的。**北京大学的王勇把贸易战的结果称为“实力对比的转折点”（原帖把这句话重复引用了两次）。吴心伯写道，缓和“并非美方回心转意或释放善意的结果”，是“中国通过艰苦奋斗和斗争赢来的”。

**第三，他们把美方的和解归功于特朗普个人。**陈东晓提到特朗普“鲜明而独特的决策风格”；赵明昊写道，特朗普“崇拜实力，更愿意和强国领导人打交道”。还有人写道，特朗普会“轻易牺牲”台湾。这些学者希望把当前的稳定锁定下来，按习近平的说法，维持三年乃至更久。

美国这一年做了哪些让步

**第四点，Doshi 说特朗普到目前为止确实一直在迁就。**对华关税一直远低于上限；暂停把中国企业列入实体清单，停的时间是 2008 年以来最长的一次；放先进 AI 芯片流向中国和它在东南亚的中转渠道；搁置对台军售，在日本和菲律宾受到中国施压时只给了有限的支持。

北京在抽梯子，也在周边加压

**第五点，北京正在“抽掉技术梯子”，为下一轮较量做准备。**他举了五类措施：

  • 国务院新规，威胁那些与中国伙伴切断关系的外国企业；
  • 出境限制，把关键人才和家属留在国内；
  • 对制造设备实施出口管制，防止别人追上来；
  • 以国家安全为由管理科研发表，保护本土技术诀窍；
  • 稀土许可证要求外国买家交出客户名单和生产细节，等于给了中国一张供应链卡点地图。

**第六点，北京趁美国迁就，在亚洲打破了以往的先例。**中国海警船员在仁爱礁附近用棍棒殴打一名菲律宾水手；在习近平国事访问华盛顿期间阻拦了一次补给任务，打破了一项维持两年的协议。日本正面对十多年来范围最广的胁迫行动，部分日本制造商面临生存威胁。在台湾周边，解放军发射火箭弹的位置比以往任何时候都更靠近台湾岛。Doshi 说，华盛顿基本上袖手旁观。

稀土交付只有承诺的三分之二

第七点是他所说的北京正在违反釜山协议。按他的说法，华盛顿履行了协议条款，暂停了“关联方规则”（Affiliates Rule），下调了关税；北京却收紧了对美国的稀土矿物和磁体供应。

美国官员说，中国只交付了承诺量的大约三分之二，贸易数据显示缺口可能更大。钇用于喷气发动机和燃气轮机，钇短缺已经迫使一些供应商停产；芯片制造商的钪也开始短缺。

Doshi 的解读是，北京相信实力换来了缓和，更多实力就能维持缓和，而且不会招致反弹。他的文章标题《中国是否误判了形势？》针对的就是这种判断。

深圳峰会：别指望突破，至少要宣布延长休战

第八点是他对深圳会面的预期。双方不太可能在深圳挽救休战，农业、航空甚至 AI 都很难有突破，尽管这些领域都有需要。但两位领导人至少应该宣布，双方正在努力，争取在 12 月迈阿密会面前延长休战。

他最后写道，如果做不到，紧张持续上升，局面失控升级的风险会越来越大。

跟踪中美关系和稀土供应链的读者，可以按 Doshi 给出的时间表盯三件事：周四深圳会面有没有提到延长休战，12 月迈阿密会面，以及 1 月 10 日休战到期。帖中稀土交付约三分之二等说法来自美国官员和 Doshi 的引述，本站没有打开《外交事务》全文核对。

Is China Misreading the Moment? (thread summary)

Trump and Xi will meet in Shenzhen Thursday. In today’s @ForeignAffairs, I argue that the so-called “detente” they struck a year ago is now under severe strain. Busan is cracking over rare earths, a new Democratic Congress will check Trump, and the Nvidia scandal is triggering new export controls. After a year of calm, we may be in for a period of tension.

Beijing has used the detente to press its advantage. It is locking in its industrial leadership, coercing its neighbors, and keeping rare earth flows tight to keep the U.S. on a tight leash. Meanwhile, the U.S. has been accommodating.

But Beijing is overreaching. If the United States believes Beijing will throttle rare earths no matter what it does, there is little reason for continued restraint. With the trade truce expiring January 10, time is running out for the two leaders to extend or preserve it. The meeting in Shenzhen, and the next one in Miami in December, are the last few opportunities to stabilize.

--

A summary of the key points:

  1. Chinese scholars see the current detente as temporary. Chen Dongxiao calls the detente a “tactical truce.” Wu Xinbo said it was “tactical stability” that is “neither sustainable nor solid.” And Shen Weizhong, a former Chinese foreign ministry official, says: “The more time we have, the better prepared we might be towards a final showdown on Taiwan or maybe another trade war.”

  2. Chinese scholars believe China’s strength built the detente, not diplomacy. Wang Yong called the trade war “a tipping point in terms of strength.” Wu writes it “is not the result of the U.S. side having a change of heart or showing goodwill, but was won by China through arduous striving and struggle.” Wang Yong of Peking University calls the trade war’s outcome “a tipping point in terms of strength.”

  3. Chinese scholars credit Trump personally for American conciliation. Chen Dongxiao points to his “distinct and unique decision-making style.” Zhao Minghao writes that Trump “admires power and prefers to engage with the leaders of powerful countries.” Others write he would "readily sacrifice" Taiwan. They want to lock in the current stability, as Xi declared, for three years and beyond.

  4. Trump has indeed been accommodating so far. He kept tariffs on China well below the cap. He paused entity listings for the longest period since 2008. He let advanced AI chips flow to China and its Southeast Asian cutouts. And he held up arms sales to Taiwan and offered only muted support to Japan and the Philippines under Chinese pressure.

  5. Beijing is pulling up the tech ladder and preparing for the next fight. New State Council orders threaten foreign firms that cut ties with Chinese partners. Exit bans keep key talent and their families in the country. Export controls on manufacturing equipment keep others from catching up. National security regulations on scientific publication protect domestic know-how. And rare-earth licenses force foreign buyers to hand over customer lists and production details, giving China a map of chokepoints.

  6. Beijing is taking advantage of American conciliation to press its advantages in Asia, breaking past precedent. A China Coast Guard crew clubbed a Philippine sailor near Second Thomas Shoal. They blocked a resupply mission during Xi’s state visit to Washington, breaking a two-year agreement. Japan now faces the broadest coercion campaign in more than a decade, existentially threatening some of its manufacturers. And around Taiwan, the PLA fired rockets closer to the island than ever before. Washington has largely stood by.

  7. Beijing is breaking the Busan agreement. Washington has complied with the terms of the Busan agreement, suspending the Affiliates Rule and lowering tariffs, but Beijing has throttled the supply of rare-earth minerals and magnets to the United States. U.S. officials say China has delivered only about two-thirds of what it promised, and trade data suggest the shortfall is larger still. Shortages of yttrium, used for jet engines and gas turbines, have forced some suppliers to suspend production. Chipmakers are running short of scandium. Beijing believes strength built detente, and more strength will preserve it - not backfire.

  8. The way forward at Shenzhen. The two sides are unlikely to save the truce at Shenzhen. We should expect few breakthroughs on agriculture, aviation, or even AI despite the need. But the leaders should at least announce they are working to extend the truce ahead of the December meeting in Miami.

If they fail, and tensions mount, there is a growing risk of runaway escalation. https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/china-misreading-moment
